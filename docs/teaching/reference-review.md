# Reference review and revision decisions

Reference: Anthropic, [Claude Code modernizes a legacy COBOL codebase](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OwMu0pyYZBc), 4:15. Reviewed its complete public English captions and representative video frames across both phases. Automatic captions misrecognize some product and routine names; they are not copied into the new script. Original reference media remains only in ignored local analysis artifacts.

## What informs this revision

- At 0:19, the lesson moves from the initial codebase into discovery and documentation.
- Around 1:13, it focuses on a particular program, then shows concrete documentation and dependency artifacts.
- At 2:22, a phase transition introduces migration and verification.
- Around 3:30, it moves into execution and comparison evidence.
- The visual sequence alternates focused terminal views with readable source and generated documents, using a consistent background and reframing to direct attention.

These are teaching and editing techniques to adapt. The replacement lesson uses original wording, our repository, and our own recordings. It does not reuse the reference's narration, footage, or claims of universal fidelity.

## Changes from the rejected first draft

Use an actual person's voice. Extend the lesson to approximately 10–12 minutes, letting the recording determine the final runtime. Introduce one idea at a time, then show it. Hold on prompts before execution and results afterward. Use a reproducible defect and repair to explain the value of the oracle. Present short, selected report fields instead of a scrolling JSON dump. Use a readable contract summary rather than a wrapped Markdown table. Caption the final human recording after it is supplied; do not reuse timing from the synthetic track.

## Completed edit

The creator supplied eight voice recordings. The finished 11:22 lesson combines selected takes with original Codex CLI footage, conceptual diagrams, and actual execution evidence. Captions were rebuilt from the edited voice track. No synthetic narration or reference footage is included. Original takes are preserved; the first synthetic video is superseded.

## Selected Cedar edition

After reviewing the human version, the creator requested an AI narrator and selected Cedar from two short OpenAI speech auditions. The resulting 9:00 edit uses AI-generated Cedar narration. Real CLI footage, the reference-inspired teaching structure, and actual verification evidence are retained. Captions and screen transitions were rebuilt around the generated delivery, with pauses for reading prompts and results. No reference footage or reference narration is reused.
