# Human narration recording

Human edition completed: eight numbered M4A takes were supplied and edited into the 11:22 lesson. Originals remain untouched in `artifacts/teaching/revision/voice-takes/`. The subsequently selected Cedar edition uses AI-generated narration; no additional human recording is required. The guidance below is reusable for future lessons.

Use the [revised narration script](narration.md). The lesson now follows the [interactive Codex questions](explore-with-ai.md), including a real candidate failure and repair in a disposable clone.

Record an actual person's voice in a conversational teaching style. Explain it as though one developer is sitting next to you. Let questions sound like questions, and allow a pause before revealing a result. There is no need to fit a fixed two- or three-minute limit.

Read the eight numbered sections as one recording, or provide eight numbered takes. Skip headings and bracketed stage directions. If a sentence goes wrong, pause and read it again; that can be edited. A quiet-room phone recording is sufficient to begin. Keep the original file without background music or aggressive noise processing.

Accepted handoff: WAV, M4A, or MP3. Provide the voice recording alongside any wording changes. The final video will follow the recording's natural pace, with extra reading time at the prompts, failure report, and repaired result. Captions will be rebuilt for that exact recording.

The finished MP4 and captions are listed in the [teaching guide](README.md). The original synthetic soundtrack was not reused.
